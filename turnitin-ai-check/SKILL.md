# Turnitin AI Detection — Interpreting the Report

Understand Turnitin's AI writing detection — what the scores mean, how to respond, and where false positives come from.

## Reading the report

Turnitin's AI indicator reports:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **AI-generated** % | Text likely written entirely by AI |
| **AI-generated + AI-refined** % | Includes AI-edited/human-edited text |
| **Highlights** | Which passages triggered detection |
| **Confidence** | How certain the model is |

**Key**: the top-line number is not a verdict. Read **which passages** were flagged.

## Score interpretation

| Score | Typical reading | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **< 20%** | Likely human or minor AI assistance | Note it; usually no action |
| **20–40%** | Mixed / possible AI editing | Look at flagged passages; ask author |
| **40–60%** | Substantial AI use likely | Strong signal; verify with process evidence |
| **> 60%** | Mostly AI-generated | Strong case; still confirm (false positives exist) |

## False positives (important)

Turnitin flags more than just pure AI text. These raise false positives:

- **Non-native English writers** — simpler sentence patterns
- **Formulaic writing** — legal, technical, scientific abstracts
- **Translated text** — machine-translated then lightly edited
- **Heavily edited AI-assisted work** — human wrote it, AI cleaned it up
- **Templates and boilerplate** — standard phrases in a genre

If the author is a non-native speaker or the text is formulaic, **treat the score as weaker evidence**.

## Evidence hierarchy (strongest first)

1. Author's admission of AI use
2. Version history showing copy-paste of AI text
3. Voice inconsistent with prior work
4. Turnitin score alone
5. "It looks like AI" by eye

Turnitin is **#4** — useful signal, not proof.

## How to respond to a flag

1. **Read the flagged passages** — do they show AI tells (uniform rhythm, hedging, no specifics)?
2. **Compare to prior work** — same voice? Same vocabulary?
3. **Check for process** — drafts, outlines, notes, revision history
4. **Talk to the author** — give them a chance to explain
5. **Decide with policy** — what does your institution's AI policy say?

## Writing up the review

Include:
- The Turnitin report (score + flagged passages)
- Your analysis of the passages
- Process evidence (or its absence)
- Author's response
- Conclusion tied to the policy

**Don't**: write "Turnitin says X, therefore guilty." Write "Turnitin flagged these passages; here's my analysis; here's the policy."

## Policy notes

- Many institutions **allow AI assistance** — check before penalizing
- Apply the rule consistently to all students
- Give a chance to respond
- Focus on learning (redo, oral defense) over punishment when possible

## Limits

- Turnitin does not prove authorship
- Detection models change as AI evolves
- No score is 100% reliable on its own
